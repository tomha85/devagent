# DevAgent Commercial Direction

DevAgent's open-source core is intended to remain useful on its own. Commercial value should be created primarily through qualification depth, private compatibility intelligence, customer workflow, evidence history, enterprise integrations, deployment, and support rather than by removing the usefulness of the public core.

This document describes product direction. It is not a promise that every item listed below is currently available as a paid product.

## Open-source core

The MIT-licensed repository may provide the common engineering engine used for:

- software-engineering evidence and verification;
- PLC engineering review for supported vendors;
- requirement, risk, regression, FAT, evidence, and report workflows;
- read-only OPC UA commissioning assistance;
- provider-neutral AI/model selection;
- local-first execution and public qualification.

See [OPEN_SOURCE.md](OPEN_SOURCE.md) for the public/private asset boundary.

## Commercial moat

Commercial differentiation should be built from assets that become more valuable through real deployments and accumulated engineering experience, including:

1. real-world compatibility intelligence;
2. private qualification corpora;
3. customer evidence history and institutional memory;
4. certified vendor/version qualification packs;
5. customer-specific semantic and engineering packs;
6. enterprise evidence registries, approvals, and audit workflows;
7. private/on-premises deployment and administration;
8. enterprise integrations;
9. production support, qualification assistance, and SLAs;
10. trust and reputation built from reproducible evidence.

Source code alone should not be treated as the primary long-term moat.

## Candidate commercial offerings

Future offerings may include:

### Certified qualification packs

Versioned compatibility and qualification packages for supported PLC/vendor environments, with explicit evidence, known limitations, and support boundaries.

### Enterprise evidence registry

A team or organization service for storing and relating project revisions, requirements, FAT evidence, regression results, commissioning evidence, approvals, and release-readiness history.

### Private deployment

Customer-controlled deployment for environments where PLC source, runtime evidence, or engineering data must remain inside an enterprise network, VPC, or on-premises infrastructure.

### Enterprise integrations

Integrations with customer engineering systems, issue trackers, CI/release systems, document repositories, identity providers, reporting workflows, or site-management systems.

### Support and qualification services

Commercial support, compatibility assistance, private qualification, onboarding, and service-level commitments for customers who require production support rather than community support.

## Data and corpus policy

Customer projects, customer runtime evidence, confidential field failures, and non-redistributable vendor artifacts must not be used as public examples merely because the core is open source.

Private corpora should be governed by customer agreements, data-handling policy, access controls, retention rules, and explicit rights to use or derive compatibility intelligence.

Where anonymized or derived data is used, the organization must still have the contractual and legal right to use that data for the intended purpose.

## Licensing principle

Code already published in this repository under MIT remains available under those terms. Separately distributed commercial software, private services, datasets, qualification packs, or hosted/on-premises enterprise components may use separate commercial terms when they are not released here under MIT.

Any future licensing change should be explicit, prospective, legally reviewed, and should not misrepresent the rights already granted for prior MIT releases.
